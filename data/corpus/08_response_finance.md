# Response mechanisms, finance and early warning
> Course demo note. Compiled from public UNFCCC, UN and Green Climate Fund material.

Three institutional mechanisms matter for climate displacement response.

The Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage, established in 2013, created the Task Force on
Displacement, whose 2018 recommendations are the main formal UNFCCC guidance on human mobility. The
loss and damage fund agreed at COP27 in 2022 and operationalised at COP28 in 2023 is the funding
instrument attached to this track. Pledges announced at COP28 were on the order of several hundred
million US dollars, which is orders of magnitude below the loss and damage cost estimates in the
academic literature, generally quoted in the hundreds of billions per year by 2030.

Early warning is the highest-leverage intervention in cost-benefit terms. The UN's "Early Warnings for
All" initiative, launched in 2022, targets universal coverage by the end of 2027, on the basis that a
substantial share of the global population — particularly in Africa and small island developing states
— remains outside effective multi-hazard warning coverage. Evaluations of early warning investment
consistently report benefit-cost ratios well above one, and Bangladesh's cyclone programme is the
standard illustration.

Anticipatory action is the operational development that follows from better forecasting. Instead of
funding a response after a disaster is declared, pre-agreed triggers release funds before the hazard
arrives, allowing evacuation support and cash transfers to reach households in the window when they can
still act. Pilots run by OCHA and the Red Cross movement in Bangladesh, Somalia and Nepal report that
pre-arrival cash reduces distress asset sales and shortens displacement duration.

The persistent structural gap is that finance is organised around either emergency humanitarian
response or long-horizon adaptation, while planned relocation sits between the two. Relocation needs
multi-year funding for land acquisition, tenure resolution and livelihood reconstruction — a profile
that neither humanitarian appeal cycles nor adaptation project finance is designed to provide.
