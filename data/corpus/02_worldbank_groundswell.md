# World Bank Groundswell — internal climate migration projections
> Course demo note. Summarised from World Bank, "Groundswell Part 2: Acting on Internal Climate
> Migration" (2021). Source: https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/handle/10986/36248

The Groundswell reports model *internal* climate migration — movement within national borders — under
scenarios combining climate outcomes with development pathways. Groundswell Part 2 (2021) extends the
2018 analysis from three regions to six: Sub-Saharan Africa, East Asia and the Pacific, South Asia,
North Africa, Latin America, and Eastern Europe and Central Asia.

The headline projection is that up to 216 million people could move within their own countries by 2050
under a pessimistic scenario of high emissions and unequal development. Sub-Saharan Africa accounts
for the largest share of this total, followed by East Asia and the Pacific and South Asia.

The mechanism modelled is slow-onset, not sudden-onset: water availability, crop productivity and sea
level rise change the habitability of places gradually, altering the population distribution that
economic geography would otherwise predict. This distinguishes Groundswell from IDMC's disaster
displacement counts, which capture abrupt hazard events. The two datasets are not additive and should
never be summed.

The report identifies emerging "hotspots" of both out-migration and in-migration that begin to appear
as early as 2030 and intensify by 2050. Its central policy claim is that the 216 million figure is not
a forecast but a scenario: under an optimistic scenario with reduced emissions and inclusive
development, the projected number falls by up to 80 percent. Migration under this framing is treated
as an adaptation strategy to be planned for, not a failure to be prevented.

Two limitations are stated explicitly. The model covers internal movement only, so cross-border flows
are outside scope. And it excludes conflict, which in practice interacts strongly with climate stress
in the Sahel and the Horn of Africa.
